Abstract

Safe intent recognition for assistive robots remains challenging when integrating neural, muscular, and gaze signals, particularly under sensor degradation or failure. TriSaFe-Trans is a lightweight tri-modal transformer that fuses electroencephalography (EEG), electromyography (EMG), and eye-tracking (ET) using modality-specific temporal encoders and reliability-gated cross-modal attention. The model is trained with multi-objective self-supervised pretraining followed by supervised fine-tuning to decode REST vs. ACTION and five assistive task classes from fixed-length windows. The system is evaluated using a balanced-window leave-one-subject-out protocol, sensor-dropout scenarios, and policy-level safety metrics that quantify unsafe actions, missed intents, useful actions, and safe idle behavior.

The central idea is not only to classify intent, but to decide when the robot should safely execute or stop.

Dataset and Data Collection

EEG

8-channel Enobio EEG recorded at 250 Hz to capture neural intent cues.

EMG

4-channel wireless surface EMG from upper-limb muscles to capture residual muscle activation.

Eye Tracking

Pupil Labs Neon eye tracking to capture gaze, pupil, validity, and visual attention cues.

The task set includes REST plus five ADL-style tasks: T1 Clock, T2 Bottle, T3 Fan, T4 Plant, and T5 Wave. Each participant completed repeated trials with pre-rest, cue, movement, and post-rest periods.

TriSaFe-Trans Method

Model Architecture

EEG, EMG, and eye-tracking windows are processed by modality-specific encoders. Their token sequences are fused by a reliability-gated cross-modal transformer. The fused representation jointly predicts action and task, and the learned modality gates are reused by the safety policy layer.

EEG Encoder

Dilated temporal convolution, BiGRU, and projection for neural temporal patterns.

EMG Encoder

Compact temporal encoder for muscle activation and envelope dynamics.

ET Encoder

MLP and BiGRU branch for gaze, pupil, validity, and attention-related features.

Safety Policy

P0–P2 convert model confidence, reliability, and uncertainty into execute/stop decisions.

End-to-End Pipeline

Synchronization

Align EEG, EMG, and ET streams using LSL timestamps and a common 250 Hz time base.

Preprocessing

Apply deterministic EEG, EMG, and eye-tracking cleaning with quality masks.

Windowing

Use 2 s windows with 0.25 s stride and majority-vote labels for action/task prediction.

LOSO Evaluation

Hold out subjects for subject-independent validation and test evaluation.

Interpretation. P0 uses the raw action probability. P1 adds sensor-status reliability and becomes different from P0 under sensor-failure scenarios. P2 is the most conservative policy because it also down-weights the execution score by uncertainty. Therefore, P2 reduces unsafe executed actions and improves safe-idle behavior.

The paper also reports single-modality dropout experiments for S1–S3 and shows that EEG, EMG, and eye tracking provide complementary information for robust assistive intent recognition.

Code and Reproducibility

The repository contains the notebook pipeline for data preparation, synchronization, labeling, LOSO split generation, preprocessing, feature extraction, training, and evaluation. Run notebooks phase by phase.

Phase 1

Data cleaning, subject scanning, and tri-modal synchronization.

Phase 2

HSMM-based REST/ACTION labeling and label export.

Phase 3–6

Manifest, TRUE LOSO splits, preprocessing, feature extraction, and model training.
